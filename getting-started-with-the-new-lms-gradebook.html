Getting Started With the New LMS Gradebook

A walkthrough on 17 September introduces the redesigned gradebook interface ahead of its full rollout next semester.

By the Learning & Teaching Office 17 Sept 2026
Image goes here
images/placeholder-lms-gradebook.jpg

Ahead of its full rollout next semester, the Digital Learning team is running a hands-on walkthrough of the redesigned LMS gradebook on 17 September, aimed at giving lecturers a chance to get comfortable with the new interface before it becomes the default across all modules.

The redesign responds directly to two of the most common complaints raised about the current gradebook: that grade calculations involving weighted categories were difficult to set up correctly, and that students frequently misunderstood what a given mark meant relative to their final grade until far later in the semester than they should have.

The redesign followed a review of support tickets logged against the current gradebook over the past two years, which showed weighted-category setup and post-hoc mark adjustments accounted for well over half of all gradebook-related queries the Digital Learning helpdesk received. Both were prioritised accordingly in the new interface, ahead of more cosmetic changes originally proposed for the redesign.

What's changing

The new interface introduces a visual weighting builder that shows lecturers, in real time, how a proposed grading scheme translates into a student's final mark as categories and weights are adjusted — replacing a setup process that previously required manually entering percentage weights with limited feedback on whether they added up correctly or produced the intended result. On the student-facing side, a running "grade so far" indicator now updates automatically as marks are released, rather than only becoming visible once all assessments for a module are complete.

The session will also cover the new bulk-adjustment tools, useful for scenarios like dropping a lowest quiz mark across a cohort or applying a moderation adjustment after marks have already been entered — both previously manual, error-prone processes in the old system.

A dry run before the deadline

Because the new gradebook becomes mandatory for all modules starting next semester, the walkthrough is structured as a practical dry run rather than a features overview — attendees are encouraged to bring their own module's grading scheme and rebuild it live in the new system with support on hand, rather than trying to figure it out alone once the deadline arrives.

"The weighting builder alone would have saved me an afternoon of manually checking my spreadsheet against what the old gradebook was actually calculating." — Lecturer, early access tester

The session runs on 17 September, with additional drop-in support sessions to follow in the weeks before the new gradebook goes live university-wide.
