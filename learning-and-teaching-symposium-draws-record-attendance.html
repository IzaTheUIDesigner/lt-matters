Learning and Teaching Symposium Draws Record Attendance

Over 300 academic staff gathered for the annual symposium, with sessions on assessment redesign, large-class facilitation and inclusive pedagogy.

By the Learning & Teaching Office 21 Aug 2026

More than 300 academic staff filled the Rhodes Drift venue for this year's Learning and Teaching Symposium, the largest turnout in the event's history and a marked increase on the roughly 210 attendees recorded last year. Staff travelled in from all six campuses, with delegations from every faculty represented across the day's programme.

The morning opened with a keynote on assessment redesign, followed by a full slate of breakout sessions running in parallel across three venues. Large-class facilitation drew the biggest crowds of the day, a reflection, organisers said, of how many modules across the university now run with enrolments well past 200 students.

Logistics scaled up to match the turnout: catering, seating and printed programmes were all revised upward twice in the fortnight before the event as late registrations kept coming in, and organisers ultimately moved the closing plenary to a larger hall than originally booked. Even with the adjustments, several of the more popular breakout sessions ran at standing capacity by mid-morning.

A programme built around three themes

Sessions clustered loosely around three themes: assessment redesign, large-class facilitation and inclusive pedagogy. Rather than a single unifying keynote message, the day was structured to let staff move between practical, classroom-ready sessions and longer discussion-format ones — a format piloted last year on a smaller scale that organisers say drove this year's expanded attendance.

Several presenters were themselves academic staff sharing what had worked, and what hadn't, in their own modules over the past year, rather than external speakers — a deliberate choice by the organising committee to keep the day grounded in practice specific to the university's own classrooms and constraints.

What attendees are taking back

Feedback collected at the close of the day pointed to the large-class facilitation sessions as the most immediately useful, with several attendees noting they left with at least one technique they planned to try in the coming weeks. The inclusive pedagogy strand, smaller in attendance than the other two themes, drew consistently high satisfaction scores from those who did attend, and organisers say they're considering giving it a larger room and a longer slot next year rather than treating it as the day's quieter track.

"I've sat through symposiums before where you leave with a folder of slides and nothing changes. This year felt different — smaller rooms, more actual back-and-forth, people admitting what hadn't worked for them." — Attendee, Faculty of Health Sciences

Organisers say planning for next year's symposium will begin in the new year, with early indications pointing to an even larger venue given this year's turnout.
