A Beginner's Guide to Using the LMS Analytics Dashboard

New reporting tools now let lecturers see engagement trends per module, week by week — here's how to read them and act on what you find.

By the Learning & Teaching Office 14 Aug 2026

New reporting tools rolled out to the learning management system now let lecturers see engagement trends per module, updated week by week, rather than relying on end-of-semester summaries or manual spot-checks of who's logging in. For lecturers who haven't used the dashboard yet, the interface can look busier than it needs to be at first glance — this guide walks through where to start.

The dashboard opens on a module-level overview: a simple line showing weekly active users against total enrolment, alongside a breakdown of which resource types — readings, videos, quizzes, discussion boards — are getting the most engagement in a given week. For most lecturers, this overview alone is enough to spot the two patterns worth acting on: a broad drop-off in activity across the whole cohort, or a resource that almost nobody is opening.

Where to start

New users are encouraged to ignore most of the dashboard's advanced filtering options in the first few weeks and focus on just two views: the weekly active users trend, and the per-student engagement list, sortable by last login. The second view is where early warning signs tend to surface first — a student who was logging in daily and drops to zero for a week is a far more useful signal than a single low quiz mark.

The dashboard also breaks engagement down by time of day and day of week, which several lecturers have used less for individual student tracking and more to reconsider when they release new content — shifting a weekly reading from Monday morning to Sunday evening, for instance, after noticing that's when most of the cohort was actually logging in.

Turning data into action

The most common mistake new users make, according to the digital learning team who built the training materials, is treating the dashboard as something to check once a semester rather than weekly. Trends that are actionable in week three — a resource nobody's opening, a cohort-wide dip after a long weekend — are much harder to do anything meaningful about by week eleven.

"I used to only find out a resource wasn't landing when nobody mentioned it in class and the marks came back low. Now I see it by Thursday of the week I posted it." — Lecturer, Faculty of Science

Short walkthrough sessions on using the dashboard are running throughout the semester through the Digital Learning team, with recordings available afterward for staff who can't attend live.
