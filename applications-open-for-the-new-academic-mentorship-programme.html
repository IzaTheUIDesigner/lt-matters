Applications Open for the New Academic Mentorship Programme

A structured, year-long mentorship pairing new academics with senior colleagues opens for applications this month.

By the Learning & Teaching Office 15 Aug 2026 · 3 min read

Applications open this month for a new, structured mentorship programme pairing early-career academic staff with more senior colleagues for a full academic year, replacing the informal, department-by-department mentoring arrangements that previously varied widely in how consistently they actually happened.

Unlike previous efforts, the programme sets a minimum cadence — mentor and mentee pairs are expected to meet at least once a month, with a suggested but flexible agenda covering teaching, research direction and navigating university processes in roughly equal measure across the year. Pairings are matched partly on discipline and partly on stated development goals, gathered through a short application form from both mentors and mentees.

The pilot ran across the Faculty of Science and the Faculty of Humanities last year with 18 pairs, and organisers are aiming for closer to 60 pairs university-wide in this first full rollout, drawing mentors from a pool of senior academic staff who volunteered after hearing about the pilot's results directly from colleagues rather than through a formal call for nominations.

Built on a smaller pilot

The full programme follows a smaller pilot run informally across two faculties last year, which organisers point to as the basis for the structured cadence now built into the wider rollout. Pilot participants consistently flagged that mentoring relationships without a set minimum frequency tended to fade after the first meeting or two, however well-intentioned both parties were at the outset.

The new programme addresses this with light-touch check-ins from the coordinating team at the three-month and six-month marks — not to monitor the content of conversations, but simply to confirm pairs are still meeting and to help unstick any pairing that's stalled.

What mentors and mentees get out of it

For mentees, the appeal is largely about having a single, consistent point of contact for the kind of questions that don't have an obvious place to go — how promotion criteria actually get applied in practice, how to say no to service requests without damaging a new career, how to structure a first few years of research alongside a full teaching load.

"Nobody sits you down and explains half of this stuff. Having someone whose job was literally to answer those questions for a year made a real difference to how settled I felt." — Pilot participant, mentee

Applications for both mentors and mentees are open through the Staff Development unit, with pairings expected to be finalised and the programme year beginning shortly after applications close.
