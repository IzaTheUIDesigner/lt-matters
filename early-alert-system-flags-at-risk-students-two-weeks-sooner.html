Early Alert System Flags At-Risk Students Two Weeks Sooner

A retooled early-warning dashboard now flags disengagement patterns before the first assessment is even due.

By the Learning & Teaching Office 3 min read
Image goes here
images/placeholder-early-alert-system.jpg

The university's early alert system has been rebuilt to catch signs of student disengagement roughly two weeks earlier than the previous version, according to figures released by the Student Success unit. Where the old system relied heavily on assessment marks — meaning the first meaningful flag often couldn't appear until after the first test or assignment — the updated dashboard draws on LMS login frequency, attendance data and engagement with weekly formative activities from day one of term.

The shift matters because the gap between a student quietly falling behind and a lecturer or advisor noticing has historically been one of the hardest problems to solve at scale. A student who stops logging into course materials in week two, but isn't due to submit anything until week six, could previously go unflagged for a month.

The rebuild took the better part of two semesters, much of it spent integrating engagement data that previously lived in three separate systems — the LMS, the timetabling platform and manually recorded attendance registers — into a single composite score advisors could act on without checking multiple dashboards each week. Getting that integration right, rather than the scoring model itself, turned out to be the harder engineering problem.

From weeks six to week four

In the system's pilot semester, the median time to first flag dropped from week six to week four, and for students who went on to need substantial support, the flag now typically arrives while there is still meaningful time to intervene before a first assessment. The dashboard assigns each student a composite engagement score updated weekly, visible to academic advisors and, with appropriate permissions, to module coordinators.

Importantly, the system is designed to flag patterns, not punish them — a single missed login triggers nothing, but a sustained drop across multiple signals over two consecutive weeks does. Advisors reviewing flagged cases stressed that the tool is meant to prompt a conversation, not replace one.

What happens after a flag

Once a student is flagged, their academic advisor receives a notification and reaches out directly — typically by email in the first instance, followed by a phone call if there's no response within a few days. The unit has also built a lightweight referral pathway so advisors can connect students quickly to tutoring, financial aid or counselling services depending on what surfaces in that first conversation.

"The earlier flag doesn't fix anything on its own — what it does is give us a two-week head start on a conversation we were always going to need to have anyway." — Academic advisor, Student Success unit

The system is currently active across first-year modules university-wide, with an extension to second-year modules under consideration for 2027 pending review of this year's outcomes.
