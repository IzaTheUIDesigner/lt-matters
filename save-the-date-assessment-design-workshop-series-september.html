Save the Date: Assessment Design Workshop Series, September

A four-part workshop series opens in September, walking departments through redesigning assessments from rubric to moderation.

By the Learning & Teaching Office 20 Aug 2026

A four-part workshop series on assessment design opens in September, offered by the Learning and Teaching Office and structured to walk departments through the full arc of redesigning an assessment — from first setting learning outcomes, through building a workable rubric, to moderating results consistently across a large marking team.

The series is deliberately sequential rather than a set of standalone sessions, with each workshop building on the previous one's output. Departments are encouraged, though not required, to send the same small team to all four sessions and bring an actual assessment they're planning to redesign, so the series produces a concrete, usable outcome rather than general principles alone.

The series was developed in direct response to feedback from last year's single-session assessment design workshop, which consistently drew strong attendance but left participants without enough structured follow-through to actually finish redesigning an assessment before the semester's pressures took over again. Spreading the same content across four spaced sessions, each with a concrete deliverable, is intended to solve that specific problem rather than simply repeating a popular session.

What each session covers

The first session focuses on aligning an assessment tightly to its stated learning outcomes — a step organisers say is skipped more often than expected, with many long-standing assessments having drifted from the outcomes they were originally designed to test. The second builds detailed, criterion-based rubrics from that alignment work. The third looks specifically at authentic task design, drawing on the same framework recently developed with input from five faculties. The final session covers moderation practice for teams marking at scale.

Sessions run roughly three weeks apart to give departments time to apply what they've covered before the next one, rather than compressing the whole series into a single intensive week. Facilitators will also be available briefly between sessions for departments that want a quick check-in on their rubric or outcomes draft before the next workshop, rather than waiting three weeks to find out whether they've gone off track.

Who the series is for

While open to any academic staff, the series is aimed particularly at module coordinators and teaching teams working on modules with large, multi-marker cohorts, where consistency problems tend to be most acute.

"Last time I did one of these workshops I left with slides. This series is structured so you leave with an actual rubric you can use next semester." — Attendee, previous assessment design workshop

Registration for the September series opens shortly, with places limited to keep sessions workshop-sized rather than lecture-sized.
