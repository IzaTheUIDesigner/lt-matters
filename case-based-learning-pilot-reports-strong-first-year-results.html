Case-Based Learning Pilot Reports Strong First-Year Results

A first-year pilot swapping lecture blocks for real-world case work reports higher engagement and steadier attendance through the semester.

By the Learning & Teaching Office 21 Aug 2026

A first-year pilot module that replaced roughly a third of its scheduled lecture time with structured case-based learning has reported markedly higher engagement and more consistent attendance through the semester than the module's previous format, according to figures shared by the teaching team behind the redesign.

Rather than working through content in the traditional lecture-then-tutorial sequence, students in the pilot spend alternating weeks working through realistic, discipline-specific case studies in small groups, with lecture time reserved for the material students need before tackling the next case rather than content delivered in a fixed sequence regardless of where students are.

The module runs across two lecture theatres with a combined enrolment of just over 340 first-year students, split into fixed groups of six for the case-based sessions. Each case is built around a scenario drawn loosely from the discipline's own professional practice, revised each semester so returning tutors can't simply reuse the previous cohort's worked solutions.

Why attendance held up

Attendance is often the first thing to slip in large first-year modules once the initial weeks of term pass, and the teaching team says this was the pattern they were most trying to break. In the pilot, attendance at case-based sessions stayed within a few percentage points of the opening-week figure through to the end of semester, a marked contrast to the steady, familiar decline the module had shown in previous years under a standard lecture format.

The teaching team attributes this partly to the group structure itself — students assigned to the same small group for the whole semester built a level of mutual accountability that anonymous lecture attendance never generated — and partly to the cases being deliberately built around scenarios students found genuinely engaging rather than abstract textbook examples.

What the students noticed

Feedback collected mid-semester pointed to a shift in how students described the workload — less "content to get through" and more "problems to work out" — which the teaching team sees as an early sign the format is changing how students relate to the material, not just how often they show up.

"I actually looked forward to Thursdays this semester, which is not something I ever expected to say about a first-year module." — Student, pilot cohort

A full evaluation of the pilot's impact on assessment outcomes will follow once end-of-semester results are in, with a decision on wider rollout to follow shortly after.
