Deputy Vice-Chancellors — Learning and Teaching from Across South Africa Gather to Chart the Future of Learning and Teaching in Higher Education

Delegates spent the day on one central question: how does the sector move from policy on paper to policy in practice?

By the Learning & Teaching Office 21 August 2026 · 6 min read

Deputy Vice-Chancellors for Learning and Teaching from universities across South Africa gathered this week for a national forum focused on one central question: how does the sector move from policy on paper to policy in practice? The room was full, engagement was active throughout, and the conversation ranged from institutional pain points to the fast-moving question of artificial intelligence in higher education.

Unlike the internal meetings DVCs navigate within their own institutions, this forum offered something different — a space where leaders carrying comparable responsibilities, but operating in different contexts, could compare notes candidly. That distinction mattered. Several delegates noted that the value of the day lay less in formal presentations and more in the frankness of the exchange between peers.

Naming the challenges honestly

A significant portion of the forum was given over to DVCs laying out, in direct terms, the difficulties their universities are currently facing in the learning and teaching space. These are challenges that rarely surface in official communications but shape academic life on the ground — from capacity constraints to the practical friction between what policy documents say and what actually happens in departments and classrooms.
