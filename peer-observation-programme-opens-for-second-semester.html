Peer Observation Programme Opens for Second Semester

Colleagues can now sign up to observe and be observed in a confidential, developmental peer review process, with training provided beforehand.

By the Learning & Teaching Office 12 Aug 2026
Image goes here
images/story-5.jpg

Colleagues across the university can now sign up for the second-semester round of the Peer Observation Programme, which pairs academic staff to observe and be observed in a confidential, developmental process explicitly separated from any formal performance review. Registration opened this week and will run for the next fortnight before pairings are finalised.

The programme, now in its third semester, works on a simple structure: each participant is matched with a colleague — usually from a different department, by design — and the pair observes one of each other's classes over the course of the semester, followed by a structured debrief conversation. Nothing from the observation is recorded or reported upward; the entire point, organisers stress, is a space low-stakes enough that both parties can be genuinely honest.

The programme launched two semesters ago with just under 40 participants and has grown each round since, now sitting at close to 90 staff signed up across the first two semesters combined. Organisers attribute the growth almost entirely to word of mouth between colleagues rather than any formal promotion, which they take as a reasonably strong signal that participants are finding the exchanges worthwhile enough to recommend to others.

Training before the first observation

New participants attend a short training session before their first observation, covering how to give specific, non-judgemental feedback and how to structure the post-observation conversation so it stays developmental rather than evaluative. Past participants have said this training — more than the observation itself — is what makes the difference between a useful exchange and an awkward one.

Pairings deliberately cross departmental and, where possible, faculty lines. The reasoning is that a colleague from a different discipline notices different things — pacing, clarity of instructions, how questions are handled — precisely because they're not distracted by the content itself.

What keeps people coming back

Uptake has grown steadily each semester by word of mouth rather than active recruitment, with several participants returning for a second or third round with a different partner. Organisers point to this repeat participation as the clearest sign the format is working.

"I've been teaching for eleven years and nobody had sat in my class and just watched, no agenda, in almost a decade. It's a strange kind of feedback you don't get any other way." — Returning participant, Faculty of Humanities

Staff interested in participating can register through the Learning and Teaching Office before the sign-up window closes at the end of the month.
